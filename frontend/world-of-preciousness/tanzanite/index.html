A gemstone like no other

One hill on earth, and nowhere else.

In a moment of awe, the Maasai of Tanzania beheld a wonder that changed the jewellery industry. A lightning bolt struck the earth and lit the rocks with a dazzling blue radiance — and tanzanite, rare and breathtaking, entered the world.

Trichroic · 01

Emerald cut

Tanzanite's allure lies in its trichroic properties: a range of hues from blue to violet-blue, and even hints of burgundy. One stone, four accounts, depending where you stand.

Trichroic · 02

Oval cut

Word spread like wildfire. A stone a thousand times scarcer than diamond, from one source only, which is why it is called a one-generation stone.

Trichroic · 03

Trillion cut

Its genesis lies in the pan-African movement of tectonic plates. The Maasai recognised its exceptional nature, sensing a potent energy in the stone.

Trichroic · 04

Marquise cut

For the Maasai it was more than a precious stone; it symbolised their link to the earth and the beauty it holds. In lithotherapy it is said to foster awareness and inner harmony.

Merelani

At the foot of Kilimanjaro

The only source of this stone is a few square kilometres of hillside in northern Tanzania. Shafts follow the seam down by hand.

Experts put the supply at a few more decades. Everything cut from it now is, in the end, finite.

Gem story

Origins of the Blue Jewel

Tanzania, a nation steeped in lore, tells it as the Maasai do: a thunderbolt came down from the heavens, struck the earth, and transmuted the rock into a glistening blue. That was the birth of tanzanite.

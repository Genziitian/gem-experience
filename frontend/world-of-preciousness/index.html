A Stone is Read in Six Ways

A stone is read in six ways, and felt in one.

The allure of a precious stone lies in its colour, tone, saturation, inclusions, character and clarity. Alongside colour, cut, clarity and carat weight sit a stone's symbolism and its personality.

The Science of Colour

Cut Proportions & Light

The depth, angles and symmetry of a stone's facets decide how light moves through it. When white light enters, some rays are reflected outward while others are absorbed, creating its body colour, and the rest are returned to the eye.

Three Dimensions of Colour

Colour is hue, tone and saturation, best pictured as a globe. The most desirable colour sits on its equator: a medium tone with high saturation.

Pleochroism

A doubly refractive gem can show different colours from different angles, as tanzanite does. A skilled lapidary cuts it so the full colour shows face up, with little or no secondary hue.

Origins of the Blue Jewel

One hill at the foot of Kilimanjaro.

The exclusive source of this captivating gemstone is confined to a tiny area nestled at the base of Mount Kilimanjaro. Tanzania, a nation steeped in lore, is home to an age-old Maasai legend where a thunderbolt descended from the heavens, hitting the earth and transmuting all the rocks into a glistening blue hue: the birth of Tanzanite.
